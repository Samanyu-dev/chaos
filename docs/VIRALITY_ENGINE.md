# 🚀 The Virality Engine — Organic Growth Loops

This document identifies the shareable moments within Orbit (Chaos) and outlines mechanics to drive organic, word-of-mouth growth.

---

## ⚡ Shareable Moments (Visual Assets)

The most engaging visual experiences in Orbit are designed to be exported and shared on social media:

### 1. Parallel Memories Rewind (Instagram Stories / TikTok)
- **The Concept**: Export an animated reel that transitions between Lila's, Kai's, and Leo's perspectives of a shared location pin.
- **Visual Asset**: A vertical video showing three perspectives of the same geolocated pin, watermarked with:
  > `"We live inside CHAOS. orbit.social/join"`

### 2. Spontaneous Chaos Wheel results
- **The Concept**: Sharing the neon roulette wheel's spin results (e.g. "Let's sing karaoke in Akihabara until dawn").
- **Visual Asset**: A high-contrast graphic with the selected adventure card, neon accent glows, and Shibuya coordinates.

### 3. Trip Replay & Map Playback
- **The Concept**: A time-lapse visualization showing friend trails, memory posts, and geolocated bills popped open across the Shibuya grid over the course of the trip.
- **Visual Asset**: A 15-second animated map replay showing pins dropping chronologically.

## 📈 Organic Virality Loops (K-Factor Strategy)

Our goal is to achieve a **K-Factor $> 1.0$** (where every active user invites at least one friend who registers an account):

```
       [1. New User registers]
                  |
                  v
       [2. Generates Trip Planet]
                  |
                  v
       [3. App prompts: "Invite crew to populate Shibuya grid"]
                  |
                  v
       [4. SMS invites sent with custom preview links]
                  |
                  v
       [5. Friend installs via link -> auto-joins active Trip Planet]
```

- **Dynamic Invitation Previews**: When an invite is sent via SMS, the link generates a web preview displaying a map outline with active friend markers, encouraging the recipient to install Orbit to "see where the crew is panned."
